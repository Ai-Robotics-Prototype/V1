# ADDENDUM 34 — August 18, 2026 (afternoon) — PHASE F OPENS: THE HYBRID IS PROVEN, THE JOG BRIDGE IS BUILT, AND MOCK TESTING EARNS ITS KEEP TWICE
*(Appended in full. Nothing above this line was removed. The session that turned the CRI evaluation into product integration: two clients on one controller proven stable for the first time — the hybrid architecture the whole Phase-F plan rests on — followed by the dashboard-jog-over-ROS2 bridge designed, built, seam-tested byte-identical, and then vindicated by exactly the kind of bug mock testing exists to catch: a state machine that could wedge itself permanently, found and fixed before it ever met the real arm. Also: three observer bugs, one friendly-fire tmux incident, a "verified didn't mean shipped" reckoning, and a latency table that reads like a different sport from the WS jog path.)*

### Section 519: F1 scope and the seam that keeps the war chest

Phase F1 = the dashboard's jog buttons drive the arm through the CRI/JTC stack. Standing decision honored throughout: the frontend's entire hardened dead-man stack (§286/§431 — pointerup/leave/cancel/blur/hidden-tab/WS-drop all stop; hold_id+seq; server keepalive; toast-on-rejection) is **kept, not rebuilt** — only the bottom of the stack changes. Seam chosen after a full Explore of the existing jog contract: **SEAM A** — `dashboard_server.py` gains one fanout publisher on `/dashboard/jog_session_events`; a new standalone `jog_bridge` node consumes it and drives FollowJointTrajectory. Rationale: zero frontend delta, zero GIL contention on the 60 ms keepalive tick, toast/reject layer intact, and the ws|ros2 backend gate lives at the bridge (which **refuses to start if estun_driver's jog gate is open** — mutual exclusion by rejecting the second-in-line). `JOG_BACKEND` env (default `ws`), startup banner per §282 doctrine; with `backend=ws` the existing path was proven **byte-for-byte identical** (mock ros_node smoke: same topic, same payload, no new attributes leaked); with `backend=ros2` the WS jog path is fully passive (`/robot/jog_command` never even created).

### Section 520: F1.0 — the coexistence gate: two clients, one controller, PASS

The Phase-F hybrid (CRI for motion on :9001/UDP, WS for I/O/diagnostics/power on :9000) required proving the controller tolerates simultaneous clients — unproven, against a crash-history controller. The test: CRI launch holding the arm + `roboai-estun` in monitor_only (all gates closed), instrumented five minutes.

**Getting a clean instrument took three observer bugs**, each a lesson in itself: (1) QoS incompatibility — the observer's default subscription couldn't hear JSB's TRANSIENT_LOCAL publisher (fix: RELIABLE+VOLATILE matches both sides); (2) ingestion truncation — `msg.data[:200]` sliced the mode JSON mid-value, the parse exception was swallowed into `{}`, and every field read `None` ("expected True, got None" with no further clue); (3) `pgrep -c -f` matching its own bash wrapper's argv, briefly manufacturing a phantom second controller_manager. A 20 s preflight gate (both stacks visible, mode fields verified, 5 s CRI baseline 200–300 Hz) was added so a Run-1-style contaminated start (observer launched mid-relaunch, rates ramping 94→215 Hz) is structurally impossible.

**The clean run:** CRI `/joint_states` **249.8–249.9 Hz across all five buckets, worst delta +0.0%** — the WS client cost the motion channel literally nothing. WS mirror flowing (~17.3 Hz joint states, ~21.3 Hz status, 1 Hz mode), zero motion, `nc :9001` 30/30. The verdict initially printed FAIL on one row: 600 rejections classified "external" — ticking at **exactly 2.00 Hz = the driver's own 0.5 s GetIOValue poll** hitting its own monitor gate (`family='write'`, `reason='monitor_only active'`), wire-silent, with no external writer in existence. Reclassified as the known-benign internal baseline signature. **F1.0: PASS. The hybrid architecture is real.** Bonus figure: across all the observer retakes, the two clients ran simultaneously attached for well over twenty minutes total without a single disconnect on either channel.

**Side-finding with a Phase-F work item:** during coexistence, move_group spammed `Joint 'joint_1..6' not found in model 'S10-140'` — its planning-scene state monitor ingesting estun_driver's WS-casing `/joint_states` stream. Absent on mock with estun_driver down, confirming coexistence-ingestion rather than a config bug. Hygiene item: point move_group's monitor at the JSB stream only (harmless for jog — jog_bridge talks to JTC, not move_group).

### Section 521: F1.1 — the bridge, its physics, and the "verified didn't mean shipped" reckoning

`jog_bridge` (ament_python, CRI workspace; production home under cobot_ws/systemd flagged for F3): a pure-Python state machine (IDLE→ACTIVE→CANCELING) driven by session events, emitting JTC goals with **goal-replacement** — press/refresh sends a fresh goal from live snapshot toward a 200 ms, limit-clamped horizon; release/silence cancels; JTC cancel = stop-and-hold on the next 4 ms tick. Timing law: 300 ms silence deadman (matches the WS driver's felt behavior), 100 ms refresh, **0.15 velocity cap** (3.3× tighter than the WS jog's 0.50), 2° limit margin enforced **in the bridge** — MoveIt is not in the jog path, and per the E4 clamp lesson nothing downstream rejects for us. The physics check that matters: **max jog per-cycle delta (0.15 × URDF vel / 250 Hz) is under the plugin's max_step_rad=0.002 for every joint** — the hardware clamp can never fire during a legal jog; it remains pure backstop. Operator ruling on the proposed "warmup" debounce state: **rejected** — the jog saga's core lesson is that clever timing layers CAUSE phantom behavior; rapid on-off is the operator's finger and is obeyed literally. Extras: `/estun/mode` drift-guard (if allow_jog flips true while backend=ros2 → FATAL, passive, cancel active session), cartesian frames refused with surfaced reason (F1 is joint-jog only), 30 unit tests green at first build.

**The reckoning:** a 90 ms fanout rate-limiter (coalescing keepalive-cadence refreshes so JTC isn't preempted 10×/s; start/stop never limited) was operator-approved, and the seam smoke test *reported it verified* ("3 press/refresh + 1 stop → 3 emissions, limiter dropped the 2nd refresh"). The F1.3 review found **the shipped SM had no limiter** — the smoke had tested a design that never landed. Reconciled by implementing it properly with a pinned regression replaying the exact smoke sequence, plus the test that matters most: **the limiter never gates a limit-approach cancel** (delaying a safety-adjacent stop by 90 ms would be a defect). The meta-lesson joins the deploy-truth family: "verified" must mean *shipped and grepped in the installed copy*, not demonstrated against intent.

### Section 522: F1.2 — the scenarios, and the bug that justified the whole mock phase

Five scenarios against real JTC (mock hardware, real arm safed via teardown): press-hold-release, keepalive-kill, limit-approach, direction-flip, rapid-tap.

**S3 found the release-blocking bug.** The limit-approach cancel raced a goal that had already completed; on ROS2 Humble, JTC's cancel response for an already-terminal goal returns without the result callback ever firing — the SM entered CANCELING and **never left**. Every subsequent press rejected with "canceling — retry" until process restart. On the real arm this would have presented as "the jog buttons stopped working," §431-style, with no recovery short of a restart. **Fix, layered:** (1) cancel-response callback inspecting return code and goal status → convergence in ~10–50 ms on the known race; (2) a **500 ms tick deadman**: any CANCELING state that hasn't converged is force-finalized to IDLE with `reason=cancel_timeout` and a loud log — the SM is now structurally incapable of wedging, whatever undiscovered race comes next. The F1.2 race is replayed verbatim as a regression; suite grew to 47/47.

**Friendly-fire incident, owned immediately:** mid-diagnosis, Claude Code sent Ctrl-C via `tmux send-keys -t robot` believing jog_bridge lived in that pane — and killed the entire mock launch (and a bare relaunch attempt then spawned a duplicate jog_bridge). It stopped, confessed, asked before touching anything else, and the operator restored the stack. Standing rule minted: **jog_bridge (and any per-session helper) never shares the tmux launch pane; the robot session belongs to the launch alone**, and anything sending keys into it must first capture-pane and read what's actually there.

**The latency table (post-fix, mock JTC):** press→first goal **4.7–12.3 ms**; refresh→preempt mean **6.6–10.5 ms**, p95 ≤ 15.8 ms; release→cancel round-trip **12.6 ms**; silence→final **326 ms** (300 ms deadman + tick slack). Rapid-tap: **5/5 clean** finals with the SM returning to IDLE every cycle — the pre-fix code wedged after tap one. For scale: the WS jog path's *release-to-wire* alone measured ≤60 ms on a good day; the ROS2 path's full press-to-goal is single-digit milliseconds, and the structural backstop underneath it all (CRI stream death = arm holds) was live-proven twice in Addenda 32–33.

## PROCESS LESSONS (227–235)
*(Numbering per the file's tail: Addendum 33 ended at 226. Claude Code's in-session drafts 227–234 are superseded by this reconciled set.)*

227. **"Verified" means shipped-and-grepped, not demonstrated-against-intent.** The 90 ms fanout limiter was approved, "verified" in a seam smoke test, and absent from the shipped state machine — the smoke had exercised a design that never landed. Every verification claim must be anchored to the installed artifact (grep the installed copy; pin the smoke sequence as a regression in the shipped test suite). Same family as the Lesson-92/deploy-truth class, one layer up: it's not just deploys that can lie, test reports can too.

228. **Any terminal state a state machine can enter, it must be able to leave — structurally, not just on the happy path.** The stuck-CANCELING race wedged jog into permanent input-rejection because CANCELING's only exit depended on a callback that (on this stack) sometimes never fires. Fix pattern: fast convergence on the known signal, plus an unconditional time-based deadman to IDLE with a loud reason. A jog system whose failure mode is "ignore the operator forever" is unshippable regardless of trigger rarity.

229. **Middleware quirks live below your abstraction: on ROS2 Humble, cancelling an already-terminal JTC goal returns a response but may never fire the result callback.** Any code awaiting goal finalization must treat "cancel acknowledged" and "result delivered" as independent events with independent timeouts. (Same epistemic class as Lesson 220's SUCCESS≠arrival: the action layer's words are not the world.)

230. **One tmux pane, one process — and never send keys into a pane you haven't just read.** The fratricide (Ctrl-C intended for jog_bridge killing the whole mock launch) happened because two processes' lifecycles were assumed to share a pane and `send-keys` fired blind. Rules minted: the robot session belongs to the launch alone; per-session helpers run in their own shells; `capture-pane` before `send-keys`, always.

231. **Instrument bugs are indistinguishable from system bugs until you cross-check with a second instrument.** Three observer defects (QoS mismatch reading 0 Hz, ingestion truncation reading None, pgrep self-match reading 2) each initially presented as system failures — a dead mirror, wrong gates, a rule violation. Each was resolved by one independent manual measurement (`topic echo`, `topic info`, `pgrep -af` with eyes on argv). Never ship a verdict from a single instrument that hasn't been cross-checked once.

232. **Ingestion must never lossily transform data; truncate at display time only.** `msg.data[:200]` at the callback sliced JSON mid-value, the parse error was swallowed, and every downstream field read None with no trace of why. Store raw; trim in the presenter; and when a parse fails, print the raw payload and the exception — the next bug should introduce itself.

233. **Classify before you count: a telemetry stream's baseline noise must be separated from actionable events, by evidence.** The 2 Hz rejection tick (the driver's own IO poll hitting its own monitor gate) flipped the coexistence verdict to FAIL until classified. The classification was then verified from payloads, source, and the wire log — not assumed from cadence alone — and recorded as a named signature so it never confuses a future session.

234. **Deterministic UX layers reject debounce: rapid on-off is the operator's finger.** The proposed "warmup" state (ignore the first 50 ms of refreshes) was rejected on §286/§431 grounds — every clever timing layer in the jog stack's history has manufactured phantom stops. Stops process immediately, always; rate-limiting applies only to redundant refreshes and never to start, stop, or any cancel.

235. **When two clients must share a controller, prove coexistence with a gated instrument before building on it — and bank the negative result path in advance.** F1.0 was designed with an explicit "if the controller objects, STOP — the hybrid needs a mediation design" branch. It passed (+0.0% motion-channel cost), but the discipline stands: architecture-bearing assumptions get their own measured gate with a pre-committed answer to failure, not a hopeful integration test after the code exists.

*Summary of Addendum 34: the day the evaluation became a product line item. The question the whole hybrid rested on — can the controller serve two masters — was answered with a flat 250 Hz and a zero-point-zero: the WS client's presence cost the motion channel nothing measurable across five instrumented minutes and twenty informal ones. Getting that number took three broken instruments, each masquerading as a broken system until one independent measurement unmasked it. Then the bridge: the dashboard's hard-won dead-man stack kept whole, a new bottom bolted on through a one-function seam proven byte-identical when idle and fully passive when active, and a state machine whose physics guarantee the hardware clamp is never more than a backstop. The fix for the second is the lesson for everything: any state a machine can enter, it must be structurally able to leave. One friendly-fire Ctrl-C took down the mock stack and minted the own-shell rule; one latency table showed press-to-goal in single-digit milliseconds where the old path measured its release alone in sixty. Nine lessons, a proposed ladder, and a bridge waiting for a finger on a button: the next time the arm moves, it will be because someone held down jog and the new nervous system answered.*
